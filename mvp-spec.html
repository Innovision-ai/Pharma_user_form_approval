01

Executive Summary

Requesting access to lab and production equipment today runs through paper forms, email chains, and verbal follow-ups with a Head of Department (HOD) and a Quality Assurance (QA) reviewer, before IT can act. There is no single record of who asked, who approved, when, or why — which makes status tracking slow and audit reconstruction painful.

This MVP replaces that process with a single web application that carries a request through Employee → HOD → QA → IT with every step, decision, and notification captured automatically. The goal of this phase is not full regulatory compliance — it is to prove the workflow, the data model, and the user experience work end to end, on real (seeded) data, so the next phase can be scoped with confidence.

What this MVP proves: that equipment access requests can move from submission to provisioning with zero manual chasing, a complete audit trail, and no step that can be skipped or approved out of order.
02

The Problem Today

Manual approval processes for regulated equipment access typically show the same failure patterns:

  • No single source of truth. Requests live in email threads, chat messages, and paper forms scattered across departments.
  • Slow, invisible approvals. An employee has no way to see whether their request is sitting with the HOD, the QA reviewer, or has quietly stalled.
  • Weak audit trail. Reconstructing who approved what, and when, for an inspection or internal review means digging through inboxes.
  • Inconsistent approver assignment. Without a maintained directory of HODs and QAs, employees may route requests to the wrong or outdated approver.
  • No role-equipment guardrails. Nothing stops a request for a role that isn't actually valid for a given piece of equipment.
03

The Proposed Solution

A single workflow, enforced by the system rather than by memory or goodwill:

Admin creates equipment→ Employee requests access→ Selects role→ Selects HOD→ Selects QA→ HOD approves→ QA approves→ IT + Employee notified→ Audit trail recorded

Every arrow above is enforced server-side: a request cannot reach QA before HOD has approved it, cannot reach IT before QA has approved it, and every transition writes an audit record automatically — nothing depends on a human remembering to log it.

04

Objectives & Scope of This MVP

The MVP is scoped to demonstrate ten capabilities, fully wired through a real database and API — not static screens:

  1. Equipment management (create, activate/deactivate, configure allowed roles)
  2. Approver management (maintain the HOD and QA directory)
  3. Employee-initiated access requests
  4. Role options that change dynamically based on the selected equipment
  5. HOD selection from a predefined, maintained list
  6. QA selection from a predefined, maintained list
  7. Sequential, enforced approval workflow (no skipped or reordered steps)
  8. Notification simulation (no SMTP required to demonstrate the flow)
  9. Full request tracking for employees, approvers, and IT
  10. A complete, unalterable audit trail of every action

The priority for this phase is functionality and a polished, professional interface — not full regulatory compliance. Section 13 lists exactly what is deliberately excluded, and Section 14 shows where that capability goes next.

07

Workflow & Status Lifecycle

Every request moves through exactly this sequence. There is no path that skips a stage.

  • Draft
  • Pending HODor Rejected
  • Pending QAor Rejected
  • Approved
  • IT Pending
  • IT Completed

A rejection at either approval stage moves the request straight to Rejected and stops it there — it does not continue automatically, and the employee is notified with the rejection reason.

Business Rules

These rules are enforced by the backend, not just the interface — a request cannot violate them regardless of what the browser sends.

#Rule
1Inactive equipment cannot be selected for a new request.
2Only roles configured for the selected equipment appear in the role list.
3Only active HODs appear in the HOD list.
4Only active QAs appear in the QA list.
5A request cannot be submitted without equipment, role, HOD, QA, and a reason.
6An HOD can only approve or reject requests assigned specifically to them.
7A QA can only approve or reject requests assigned specifically to them.
8QA cannot act until HOD approval is complete.
9IT cannot act until QA approval is complete.
10Every workflow transition creates an audit record.
11Every notification event creates a notification record.
12HOD and QA details are snapshotted into the request at submission time (see Section 9).

Data Model

Five entities carry the whole system. Field lists below are representative, not exhaustive.

ApproverThe HOD / QA directory.ID, name, type, department, email, active flag

Audit LogImmutable record of every action taken.Request ID, user, action, description, timestamp
NotificationEvery mock email the system generated.Request ID, recipient, subject, type, status

Why the HOD/QA snapshot matters

When a request is submitted, the selected HOD's and QA's name, ID, and email are copied directly onto the request — not just referenced by ID. If the Approver Master changes later (a QA leaves, a department is restructured), historical requests still show exactly who was asked to approve them at the time. This is the single most important integrity rule in the data model.

10

Notifications & Audit Trail

Notifications

The MVP does not depend on a real mail server. Every event that would normally trigger an email — request submitted, HOD approved, QA approved, access completed — instead creates a notification record: recipient, subject, and body are generated and logged as Sent, visible in the Notification Center. The service is structured so a real SMTP or Microsoft 365 connection can be dropped in later without touching any workflow logic.

Audit trail

Every state change — creation, notification, approval, rejection, completion — writes a permanent audit record with the acting user, action, description, and timestamp. Records cannot be edited or deleted from the interface, so the trail can be trusted as a record of what actually happened.

Architecture & Technology

A deliberately simple three-tier build, chosen for speed of delivery and a clean upgrade path — not because it's the ceiling of what the platform can become.

React + TypeScriptFrontend — the interface everyone uses
FastAPI (Python)Backend — enforces every business rule
SQLiteDatabase — swappable for PostgreSQL later

Demo login, not production authentication

The MVP uses a simple demo-user switcher instead of real credentials, so every role's experience can be shown in one sitting without directory integration. This is a deliberate MVP simplification, not the intended production security model — see Sections 13–14.

Guided Demo Script

Exactly how a live walkthrough runs, using seeded demo data. No manual database edits at any point.

1

Submit a request — as Employee

Log in as Yash Agrawal (Employee). Open Create Request, select HPLC System — the role list loads Analyst, Senior Analyst, Scientist automatically. Choose Analyst, select HOD Rahul Mehta, QA Priya Shah, enter a reason, and submit.

System generates REQ-0001, sets status to Pending HOD, notifies Rahul Mehta, and logs the creation to the audit trail.

2

Approve as HOD

Switch to Rahul Mehta (HOD). Open Approvals — REQ-0001 appears. Click Approve.

Status moves to Pending QA, Priya Shah is notified, audit trail updated.

3

Approve as QA

Switch to Priya Shah (QA). Open Approvals — REQ-0001 appears. Click Approve.

Status moves to IT Pending. Both the employee and the IT team are notified.

4

Provision as IT

Switch to IT Support. Open IT Requests — REQ-0001 appears. Click Mark Access Completed.

Status moves to IT Completed. Yash sees "Access Granted" and the full history on his request detail page.

5

Show the rejection path

Repeat Step 1 with a new request, then reject it as HOD (or as QA, after HOD approval) with a written reason.

Status becomes Rejected and stops — the employee is notified with the stated reason, and the audit trail shows exactly where and why it stopped.

Roadmap Beyond MVP

Round out the experience.

  • Advanced analytics & reporting
  • PDF export of requests and audit history
  • QR-code equipment tagging

Assumptions & Dependencies

  • The MVP runs on seeded, synthetic demo data — no real employee or patient data is used at this stage.
  • Real organisational data (departments, HOD/QA lists, equipment catalogue, allowed roles) will be supplied by the client before any pilot rollout.
  • Hosting environment (on-premise vs. cloud) is undecided at this stage; the MVP is designed to run locally for demonstration.
  • Scope is single-site / single-organisation; multi-site or multi-tenant use is not addressed in this phase.
  • Interface targets modern evergreen browsers (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari); legacy browsers are not supported.
  • The interface is English-language only for this phase.
  • This MVP demonstrates workflow logic — it does not constitute a validated GxP system. Production rollout requires formal validation per the client's quality system.

Risks & Mitigations

RiskMitigation
The demo login is mistaken for the intended production security model.Explicitly scoped here as MVP-only; SSO/AD integration is Phase 2 (Section 14).
Approver Master data changes after a request was submitted, causing confusion about who actually approved it.Approver Snapshot (Rule 12) freezes HOD/QA details at the moment of submission.
Repeated live demos leave the database in a messy state.Admin-only Reset Demo Data action restores the seed dataset instantly.
A request is approved out of sequence or by the wrong approver.Enforced server-side (Rules 6–9) — rejected regardless of what the interface allows.

Acceptance Criteria (UAT Checklist)

To be verified jointly at User Acceptance Testing before this document is signed off.

Equipment

  • Admin can create equipment and it receives a unique ID
  • Admin can activate / deactivate equipment
  • Allowed roles can be configured per equipment
  • Employees cannot select inactive equipment on a new request

Approvers

  • Admin can create HODs and QAs with a stored, valid email
  • Active / inactive status is respected in the HOD and QA dropdowns
  • Employee can select an HOD and a QA when submitting a request

Request & workflow

  • Employee information is auto-populated and read-only
  • Role list updates dynamically based on selected equipment
  • Request cannot be submitted without equipment, role, HOD, QA, and reason
  • Submission generates a unique Request ID and sets status to Pending HOD
  • HOD approval moves status to Pending QA; HOD rejection moves it to Rejected
  • QA approval moves status to IT Pending; QA rejection moves it to Rejected
  • IT completion moves status to IT Completed

Notifications & audit

  • A notification record is generated at every workflow transition
  • Notification history is visible in the Notification Center
  • Every action (creation, approval, rejection, completion) is logged to the audit trail
  • Audit records cannot be edited or deleted from the interface

End-to-end demo

  • The full path — Create Request → HOD Approval → QA Approval → IT → Access Completed — runs without any manual database edits
